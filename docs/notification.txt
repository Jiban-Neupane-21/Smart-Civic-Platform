# Full Notification System for Citizen Complaint Portal

The notification system should be designed as a **role-based, event-driven communication system**. The important principle is that users should only receive notifications that are relevant to their responsibilities, their municipality, their department, their assigned team, or their own complaints. A citizen should not see another citizen's complaint activity, one municipality should not receive another municipality's complaint notifications, and a staff member should not receive every notification generated by their department. The system should also distinguish between **normal notifications, important notifications, and emergency/system alerts**.

## 1. Citizen Notification System

The **Citizen** should receive notifications primarily related to their own account, complaints, complaint progress, responses, and important public alerts. When a citizen successfully submits a complaint, the system should immediately send a confirmation notification telling them that the complaint has been received, together with the complaint reference number, submission date, category, and current status. This gives the citizen proof that the complaint was successfully registered.

When the complaint is reviewed and accepted by the appropriate department, the citizen should receive a notification explaining that the complaint has been accepted and forwarded for processing. If the complaint is assigned to a department or team, the citizen can be informed that their complaint has been assigned for investigation, although sensitive internal staff information does not need to be exposed.

When the complaint changes from **Pending** to **In Progress**, the citizen should receive a status-update notification. The notification should explain that the responsible department or team has started working on the issue. If additional information is required from the citizen, the citizen should receive a notification requesting that information. This notification should ideally provide a direct connection to the complaint detail page.

When a complaint is **Resolved**, the citizen should receive a prominent notification informing them that the responsible department has completed the complaint. The citizen should be able to open the complaint and see the resolution description, resolution date, supporting evidence if applicable, and any response provided by the department. You can also allow the citizen to confirm whether the issue was actually resolved.

If a complaint is **Rejected**, the citizen must receive a notification containing the rejection reason. A rejection should never simply appear as "Rejected" without an explanation. Depending on your business rules, the citizen may be allowed to correct the issue, provide additional information, or reopen the complaint.

If a citizen **reopens a complaint**, the system should notify the citizen that the reopening request was successfully recorded. At the same time, the responsible department and municipality should receive notifications because reopening requires administrative attention.

Citizens should also receive **emergency notifications** when the municipality publishes an important public emergency affecting their area. Examples include major flooding, road closures, waste-management emergencies, public-health warnings, or other municipality-approved emergency information. These notifications should be clearly distinguished from ordinary complaint notifications.

Citizens should not receive notifications about other citizens' complaints, internal staff assignments, internal department discussions, administrative actions unrelated to them, or system-management activities.

---

# 2. Staff Notification System

The **Staff** notification system should be focused on work that the staff member or their assigned team needs to perform. Staff should not receive every notification generated by their department.

When a department assigns a complaint to a team, every relevant team member should receive a notification informing them that the complaint has been assigned to their team. The notification should contain the complaint reference number, category, general location, priority, and required action. If the system uses team-based assignment, the staff member should also be able to see the other members of their assigned team.

When a staff member becomes responsible for a particular task, the system should notify them about the assignment. For example, if a sanitation complaint requires field inspection, the relevant staff member can receive a notification indicating that the complaint requires inspection.

Staff should receive notifications when the status of a complaint they are working on changes. For example, if a department head reassigns the complaint, changes its priority, requests additional investigation, or reopens it, the assigned staff should be notified.

If a citizen provides additional information or attachments to a complaint that the staff member is working on, the assigned team should receive a notification. This prevents staff from having to repeatedly check the complaint manually.

When another staff member or team completes an assigned task, relevant team members can receive a notification when that completion affects their work. However, unnecessary notifications should be avoided.

If a citizen **reopens a resolved complaint**, the previously responsible team should receive a notification explaining that the complaint has been reopened and why, if a reason was provided.

For emergency complaints, staff should receive a high-priority notification. An emergency notification should be visually different from ordinary notifications so that staff can immediately recognize that it requires urgent attention.

Staff should not receive notifications belonging to unrelated municipalities, unrelated departments, unrelated teams, or complaints they have no authorization to access.

---

# 3. Department Head Notification System

The **Department Head** should receive notifications related to complaints, teams, staff, and activities belonging to their department and municipality.

When a new complaint is submitted and categorized for their department, the Department Head should receive a notification informing them that a new complaint requires departmental attention. The notification should identify the complaint category, location, priority, submission time, and complaint reference number.

If the complaint is particularly urgent or has been classified as an emergency, the Department Head should receive a high-priority notification. This allows the department to prioritize emergency cases instead of treating every complaint equally.

When the Department Head assigns a complaint to a team, the relevant team members should be notified, while the Department Head can retain the assignment record in their complaint history. If a team assignment changes, the affected staff should be notified and the Department Head should have an audit record of the change.

If a staff member or team changes the complaint status, the Department Head should receive a notification when that status change requires review or departmental awareness. For example, a complaint moving from **In Progress** to **Resolved** may generate a review notification.

When staff marks a complaint as completed, the Department Head can receive a notification requesting verification or approval, depending on your workflow. This is preferable to automatically considering every staff-submitted resolution final.

If a complaint is rejected, the Department Head should be notified, particularly when departmental approval is required for rejection. If a citizen later reopens the rejected or resolved complaint, the Department Head should receive a notification because the department must reassess it.

The Department Head should also receive notifications concerning departmental performance, such as complaints approaching their response deadline, overdue complaints, unusually high complaint volumes, or complaints remaining unresolved for a long period. These are not individual complaint notifications only; they are **management notifications**.

The Department Head should not receive complaints belonging to another department unless they have a specific administrative permission to access them.

---

# 4. Municipality Notification System

The **Municipality** should operate at a broader level than individual departments. Its notification system should provide visibility into municipality-wide complaint activity, departmental performance, important incidents, and administrative events.

When a citizen submits a complaint to the municipality, the municipality's responsible administrative users should receive a notification. However, this should not mean that every municipality employee receives every complaint. Notification recipients should be limited to users who have responsibility for complaint oversight.

For example, if a citizen from Municipality A submits a sanitation complaint, Municipality A's authorized users can receive the relevant notification. Municipality B should receive absolutely nothing about that complaint.

When a complaint is assigned to a department, the municipality can receive an informational notification if municipality-level oversight is required. If your system has a large number of complaints, however, sending the municipality a notification for every internal status change could create notification overload. In that situation, the municipality should receive notifications for significant events rather than every minor action.

The municipality should receive notifications when complaints remain pending for too long, when departments have a high number of unresolved complaints, when emergency complaints are reported, or when important complaints are escalated.

When a department resolves a complaint, the municipality may receive a resolution notification if municipal-level monitoring is required. The municipality should be able to see resolution statistics and complaint trends without necessarily receiving individual notifications for every minor update.

The municipality should also receive notifications related to departments and staff under its authority, such as a department being created, deactivated, having unusually high unresolved complaints, or experiencing operational problems.

For municipality-level emergency situations, authorized municipality users should receive high-priority notifications so they can coordinate the appropriate response.

---

# 5. Superadmin Notification System

The **Superadmin** should have the broadest system visibility, but this does **not** mean the Superadmin should receive every complaint notification.

That distinction is important.

If your platform grows to thousands of complaints, sending the Superadmin a notification every time a citizen submits, updates, or resolves a complaint would make the notification system almost useless. Superadmin notifications should therefore focus on **system-level, security-level, administrative, and exceptional events**.

When a new municipality is registered, the Superadmin should receive a notification. If a municipality is suspended, deactivated, modified in an important way, or requires administrative attention, the Superadmin should also be notified.

When important system configuration changes occur, the Superadmin should receive a notification. This can include changes to system settings, role permissions, security configuration, notification configuration, or other critical administrative settings.

The Superadmin should receive **security notifications** for suspicious activity. Examples include repeated failed login attempts, unusual administrative activity, unexpected permission changes, suspicious account behavior, or other events detected by your security mechanisms.

The Superadmin should also receive important **system-health notifications**, such as failures in email delivery, notification services, storage problems, database problems, or other infrastructure failures that could affect the portal.

The Superadmin can also receive **aggregated complaint alerts** rather than individual complaint notifications. For example, if one municipality suddenly receives an unusually large number of complaints within a short period, the system could notify the Superadmin about the abnormal activity.

Similarly, if a municipality has an unusually high number of unresolved complaints, the system could generate an administrative alert.

For major emergency situations affecting multiple municipalities, the Superadmin should receive an emergency notification.

The Superadmin should have access to system-wide notification history, but that does not mean every complaint should appear as a personal notification. Individual complaint information can remain accessible through authorized dashboards and audit records.

---

# 6. Municipality Isolation

This is one of the most important parts of your notification architecture.

Every complaint should be associated with a specific **municipality**, and the notification system should use that relationship when determining recipients.

For example:

**Citizen A → Municipality A → Complaint A → Department A → Team A**

The notifications generated from that complaint should stay within the authorized scope of Municipality A.

A citizen from Municipality B should not receive it.

A Department B user should not receive it.

A Municipality B administrator should not receive it.

The system should not depend on the frontend to hide those notifications. The backend and database authorization rules should determine who can access them.

This means municipality separation should exist at the **data, authorization, backend, and notification levels**, not merely at the user-interface level.

---

# 7. Department Isolation

Municipality isolation alone isn't enough.

Suppose Municipality A has:

**Sanitation Department**

**Health Department**

**Education Department**

A sanitation complaint should primarily notify the Sanitation Department, not every department.

For example:

**Citizen → Sanitation Complaint → Sanitation Department**

The Health Department should not receive it unless the complaint is intentionally assigned or escalated to Health.

This prevents departmental notification overload and protects internal information.

If a complaint needs multiple departments, the system can support an explicit **multi-department assignment or escalation**. Only then should another department begin receiving relevant notifications.

---

# 8. Team-Level Notifications

Because your portal uses **teams rather than assigning everything to a single staff member**, team notifications are important.

When a complaint is assigned to Team A, the system should notify all authorized members of Team A.

If Team A contains three staff members, all three can receive the assignment notification.

If Staff A is removed from Team A later, they should no longer receive new team notifications.

If Staff B joins Team A, Staff B can receive future notifications but should not automatically receive every historical notification unless your system explicitly allows it.

When the team completes the complaint, the relevant departmental users should receive the completion notification.

This creates a clean hierarchy:

**Complaint → Department → Team → Staff Members**

rather than:

**Complaint → Random individual staff member**

---

# 9. Notification Priority

Your system should not treat every notification equally.

I recommend three primary levels.

### Normal

Used for ordinary information.

Examples include:

Complaint submitted, complaint assigned, normal status update, profile-related notification.

### Important

Used when the user needs to pay attention.

Examples include:

Complaint approaching deadline, complaint reopened, additional information required, departmental review required.

### Emergency

Used for urgent situations.

Examples include:

Emergency complaint, disaster-related alert, major public safety event, critical system/security incident.

The interface should make these priorities visually distinguishable without relying only on color, because users may have accessibility needs.

---

# 10. Notification Categories

Rather than having one huge list, notifications can be categorized.

### Complaint

Used for:

* Complaint submitted
* Complaint accepted
* Complaint assigned
* Complaint status changed
* Complaint resolved
* Complaint rejected
* Complaint reopened

### Assignment

Used for:

* Team assignment
* Staff assignment
* Team change
* Reassignment

### System

Used for:

* System updates
* Service failures
* Configuration changes

### Security

Used for:

* Suspicious activity
* Permission changes
* Security incidents

### Emergency

Used for:

* Emergency complaints
* Public emergency alerts
* Critical incidents

### Administrative

Used for:

* Municipality changes
* Department changes
* Staff management
* Account management

This makes it easier for users to filter their notification center.

---

# 11. Notification Lifecycle

Every notification should have a lifecycle.

When an event occurs, the system determines whether a notification is required. It then identifies the correct recipient based on the user's role, municipality, department, team, and relationship to the event.

The notification is created as **unread**.

The recipient sees the notification in the notification center and notification indicator.

When the user opens or marks it as read, its state changes to **read**.

The system can retain the notification in notification history so the user can review previous notifications.

Older notifications can eventually be archived according to your application's retention policy.

This gives you a lifecycle like:

**Event → Recipient determination → Notification created → Unread → User reads → Read → History/Archive**

---

# 12. Notification Center

Each role should have a notification center, but the contents should differ according to permissions.

The notification center should provide:

**All notifications**

Shows all notifications available to that user.

**Unread**

Shows notifications that the user has not yet read.

**Important**

Shows important notifications.

**Emergency**

Shows emergency notifications.

**Complaint-related**

Shows notifications related to complaints.

The notification should ideally allow the user to click it and go directly to the relevant object, such as the complaint detail, team assignment, department, or system event.

---

# 13. In-App, Email, and Other Notifications

Your first implementation should focus on **in-app notifications**.

Once the core system works, you can add email notifications for important events.

For example, a citizen could receive an in-app notification when a complaint changes status and optionally an email for major status changes.

Emergency notifications may use stronger delivery methods depending on your future requirements.

You should avoid sending emails for every ordinary event. Otherwise, users will quickly ignore them.

A better model is:

**Normal event → In-app**

**Important event → In-app + optional email**

**Emergency event → In-app + stronger alert channel**

---

# 14. Notification vs Audit Log

Do not treat notifications and audit logs as the same thing.

A notification answers:

> **"What does this user need to know?"**

An audit log answers:

> **"What happened in the system, who performed it, and when?"**

For example, when Staff A resolves a complaint:

**Citizen notification:**
"Your complaint has been resolved."

**Department notification:**
"Complaint CMP-1001 has been marked resolved and requires review."

**Audit record:**
"Staff A changed complaint CMP-1001 from In Progress to Resolved at a specific time."

The notification is user-facing communication. The audit log is accountability and system history.

---

# 15. Recommended Overall Notification Flow

Your complete system can conceptually work like this:

**Citizen submits complaint**

→ System identifies the citizen's municipality

→ Complaint is created under that municipality

→ Appropriate department is identified

→ Relevant municipality/depart­ment users receive notifications

→ Citizen receives submission confirmation

→ Department assigns a team

→ Team members receive assignment notifications

→ Staff work on the complaint

→ Citizen receives important status updates

→ Department Head receives relevant progress/completion notifications

→ Municipality receives significant oversight notifications

→ Complaint is resolved or rejected

→ Citizen receives final notification

→ If reopened, the relevant department/team/municipality receives a new notification

→ All important actions are recorded in the audit log

At the same time, **Superadmin remains focused on system-wide events rather than every individual complaint**.

# Final Role-Based Notification Model

| Role                | Main Notification Responsibility                                                                                       |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Citizen**         | Own complaints, responses, status changes, resolutions, rejections, emergency public alerts                            |
| **Staff**           | Assigned complaints, team work, reassignment, additional information, reopened complaints, emergencies                 |
| **Department Head** | Department complaints, assignments, staff/team activity, overdue complaints, resolutions, escalations                  |
| **Municipality**    | Municipality-wide complaint activity, department performance, important complaints, emergencies, administrative events |
| **Superadmin**      | System, security, municipality administration, abnormal activity, critical errors, system-wide emergencies             |

The most important architectural rule is:

> **Notification recipients should be determined from the complaint's actual municipality, department, team, and user relationships—not from whatever municipality or role the frontend sends.**

That is what prevents **Municipality A's citizen complaint from accidentally notifying Municipality B** while still allowing Superadmin to have system-wide visibility.
